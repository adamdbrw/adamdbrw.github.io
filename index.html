Adam Dąbrowski

As CTO of Robotec.ai, Adam Dąbrowski drives the company's technology vision and strategy and directs all engineering for robot simulation and physical AI, while remaining a hands-on contributor on many projects. One of his key projects is Slingshot, which bridges high-fidelity simulation and physical AI in one end-to-end flow: a walk-through of a real site becomes a simulation-ready digital twin in days, not months.

He is an advocate for open-source ecosystems. He proposed the ROS Simulation Interfaces, a common ROS 2 interface for robot simulators now implemented in Gazebo, NVIDIA Isaac Sim and O3DE, and is their lead author. He is also lead author of REP 158, the standard for describing robot simulation assets in OpenUSD so they can move between simulators, accepted by the ROS and Gazebo project management committees.

He is well recognised in the community: an expert reviewer for the European Commission, regularly invited to give talks, keynotes and workshops, and an active promoter of new technology among younger engineers, driving agentic coding at Robotec.ai and giving volunteer guest lectures at the University of Warsaw.

Business impact: technology vision, strategy and product

  • Company growth. Among the first five people at Robotec.ai, he has led its technology through bootstrapped growth to 50 people, building simulation and digital-twin products and services for robotics and automation customers.
  • Strategy and roadmap. As CTO he owns Robotec.ai's technology strategy and roadmap: open standards and open-source collaboration as the base, and a path from services to products (the RoSi digital-twin platform, Slingshot).
  • Product. Slingshot, which he conceived and built, turns a site walk-through into a simulation-ready digital twin in days, not months: a new product line for Robotec.ai.
  • Quality at scale. The rosbag2 performance work he led took robot data recording from losing up to 70% of messages under load to keeping about 100%, shipped to every ROS 2 user from the Galactic release.

Point of view

Physical AI will be won by engineering, not only by bigger models: safety that holds “even with the dumbest AI in charge”, simulation that tests operations at scale before anything touches the floor, modular robot skills instead of monoliths, and small, additive wins first.

Today's demos deliver about 90%. The industry runs at 99.9%.
From The Blueprint for Physical AI, his keynote in the main-stage session Foundation Models in Robotics, Davos Tech Summit, July 2026 (slides, PDF)
